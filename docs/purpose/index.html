Bred for purpose

Bred for a job

Most breeds were made to do something: chase hares, find birds, herd sheep, guard the farm, or sit on a lap. Here we sort all 164 breeds by their original job and ask what their DNA says. Did each job get bred once, in one family of dogs, or many times over in unrelated ones? And which working dogs were crossed with other breeds the most?

Jobs are simplified to one per breed, from standard breed histories. Many breeds did several jobs, and plenty are mostly companions today.

One job, many families

Each row is a job. Each column is one of the family groups from the family tree, in the same order. A dot means that family has breeds doing that job, and bigger dots mean more breeds. A job spread across many columns was bred more than once, in unrelated dogs. Hover over a dot to see the breeds, over a job to see all its breeds by family, or over a family to see all its breeds by job. Click a row to explore it below.
